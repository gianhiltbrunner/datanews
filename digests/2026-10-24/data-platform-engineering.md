# AWS Lake Formation adds cross-Region fine-grained data access

AWS extended Lake Formation's governed access across regions, and ClickHouse published a migration case study with the kind of hard numbers practitioners actually want. Elsewhere, a years-old Snowflake customer-data breach reached a legal resolution, and most of the day's Google News volume was unrelated stock-ticker noise riding the word "Snowflake."

## Top stories
- **[Enable cross-Region fine-grained data access with AWS Lake Formation and Trusted Identity Propagation](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/big-data/enable-cross-region-fine-grained-data-access-with-aws-lake-formation-and-trusted-identity-propagation/)** — AWS Big Data Blog. Trusted Identity Propagation with IAM Identity Center now works across regions, so Lake Formation-governed S3 and Redshift data shared in one region can be queried from another as the actual corporate user, with per-user auditability in CloudTrail. *Why it matters:* cross-region data sharing has usually meant either duplicating governance rules or losing user-level audit trails; this closes that gap.
- **[How Shipsidekick replaced Neon with ClickHouse Managed Postgres for 10x faster queries and better reliability](https://clickhouse.com/blog/shipsidekick-neon-to-clickhouse-managed-postgres)** — ClickHouse Blog. Shipsidekick moved its production Postgres from Neon to ClickHouse Managed Postgres over a single weekend, cutting CPU usage from 100% to 5–10% and making queries roughly 10x faster. *Why it matters:* a concrete before/after migration story, with the kind of numbers that make a Postgres-hosting decision easier to justify.
- **[Hacker Linked to Ticketmaster Snowflake Breach Pleads Guilty in 165-Company Attack](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMiugFBVV95cUxPdGJiLWpwS2RrN0oyNDhmWDZ4YTg5c2FyMmlkelFiVDhINDVhLUNqWFZ2ODhPaS1IbkxOdmNxaTlGazhnNVA3MFVkU0tRUzROR3IwQTBfNjVscHZDa1lXbWZna2FrVExLOFVzbWRwel9HOTRBaDExOGZ6dmI1YlJNMDdQNVcxS2l2UTkyVjAtenpOYl9kTHJMb2Z3TzRMTUxiV2ZnOGIzSmxOWXh4SENuWlBia1phb2U1dHc?oc=5)** — TicketNews, via Google News. A hacker tied to the 2024 wave of breaches against companies using stolen Snowflake customer credentials, including Ticketmaster, pleaded guilty to attacks spanning 165 companies. *Why it matters:* a reminder that the costliest "Snowflake breach" of recent memory was a credential-stuffing problem, not a platform vulnerability — the lesson is still about MFA and credential hygiene on customer accounts.
