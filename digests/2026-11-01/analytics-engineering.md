# dbt Labs asks whether coding agents still need dbt-specific skills

dbt Labs' own benchmark is the headline: newer models already know dbt well enough that bespoke agent skills matter less than process. Alongside it, an opinion piece questions what AI-generated dashboards mean for dimensional modeling, and a few practical posts cover schema-change and snapshot pitfalls.

## Top stories
- **[Have dbt skills been bitter lesson'd?](https://docs.getdbt.com/blog/do-coding-agents-still-need-dbt-skills)** — dbt Developer Blog. dbt Labs ran Claude Opus, Sonnet and Haiku 5.5, plus Sonnet 4.5, through a Formula 1 dbt project with and without dbt agent skills, and found the 5.5 models already knew dbt well enough that run-to-run noise outweighed any skills effect; what separated good runs from bad ones was process. *Why it matters:* as models improve, teams may get less value from bespoke agent-skill packs than from disciplined workflows.
- **[dbt Charts: The Markdown of dashboards](https://roundup.getdbt.com/p/dbt-charts-the-markdown-of-dashboards)** — The Analytics Engineering Roundup (dbt Labs). Dave Fowler argues AI-generated dashboards should be a spec you can audit, and raises whether AI makes dimensional modeling less necessary as agents build dashboards on demand. *Why it matters:* a provocative framing of how semantic layers and dashboards change once agents are the ones building them.
- **[Show HN: Fitness Functions for Dbt Projects](https://github.com/tjirab/tff)** — Hacker News. A new tool, tff, adds CI-pluggable "fitness functions" for dbt projects to enforce layer boundaries and catch duplicated CTE logic, inspired by *Building Evolutionary Architectures*. *Why it matters:* tackles a real scaling pain point — DAG violations and sprawling models — with automated, CI-enforced checks.
