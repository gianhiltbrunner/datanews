# AWS says committed cloud capacity is largely sold out through 2027

A quiet day for new tooling, but the standout story is macro: AWS capacity constraints that will shape where and how pipelines run. Alongside it, two practitioner write-ups dig into lakehouse incident debugging and CDC schema-change handling, with Spark 4 and Fabric ingestion trade-offs as further reading.

## Top stories
- **[AWS Capacity Largely Sold Out Through 2027, With On Demand Headroom Reserved](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/11/aws-capacity-sold-out/?utm_campaign=infoq_content&utm_source=infoq&utm_medium=feed&utm_term=AI%2C+ML+%26+Data+Engineering)** — InfoQ. Amazon CEO Andy Jassy says AWS's large committed cloud capacity is largely sold out through 2027, with part of 2028 already allocated, and practitioners report difficulty getting On Demand capacity for ordinary EC2 instances. *Why it matters:* teams planning new pipeline or Databricks/EMR capacity on AWS may need to budget for scarcer on-demand headroom.
- **[CDC Schema Changes: What Breaks and How to Ship Them](https://dev.to/pipelinewhiteboard/cdc-schema-changes-what-breaks-and-how-to-ship-them-3678)** — DEV. Part 5 of a CDC-in-production series follows a schema change through a pipeline, covering registry rules, what Snowflake and the lakehouse can absorb, and a migration pattern meant to avoid breaking either side. *Why it matters:* silent schema drift in CDC pipelines is a common source of data loss that looks like nothing failed.
- **[Debugging a Metadata-Driven Lakehouse Pipeline](https://medium.com/@sudhanvag/debugging-a-metadata-driven-lakehouse-pipeline-a19de8489832?source=rss------data_engineering-5)** — Medium. Walks through two production incidents in a metadata-driven lakehouse pipeline that initially looked like data problems but had different root causes. *Why it matters:* a real-world reminder that metadata-driven pipeline failures often hide in the framework, not the data.
