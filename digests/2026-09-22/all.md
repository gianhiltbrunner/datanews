# dbt Labs unveils dbt v2 as Iceberg's hidden costs and failure modes take center stage

Across data engineering, analytics engineering, and data platform engineering, the strongest stories today were about lakehouse economics and reliability: what Iceberg tables actually cost to maintain, how to recover them when they break, and how Postgres-on-NVMe is reshaping the transactions-vs-analytics tradeoff. Analytics engineering's biggest news was dbt Labs showcasing dbt v2 at its annual summit, with an early migration write-up already documenting what breaks in the move to a Rust-based core.

## Top 5 across domains
- **[Analytics Engineering]** **[dbt Core v2.0 Just Rewrote Itself in Rust — Here's What Actually Breaks When You Migrate](https://medium.com/tech-with-abhishek/dbt-core-v2-0-just-rewrote-itself-in-rust-heres-what-actually-breaks-when-you-migrate-fbd16c84b666)** — written alongside dbt Labs' annual summit unveiling of dbt v2, this piece documents migration friction from the new Rust-based core, including a Fivetran tie-in and a string of early GitHub issues.
- **[Data Platform Engineering]** **[How I Fixed Corrupted Iceberg Partition Files Without Full Table Scans or Data Loss](https://medium.com/towards-data-engineering/how-i-fixed-corrupted-iceberg-partition-files-without-full-table-scans-or-data-loss-f1a328cef12f?source=rss------data_platform-5)** — a 4-step playbook for recovering Iceberg tables hit by missing Parquet magic bytes, without rescanning the whole table.
- **[Data Engineering]** **[The Real Cost of Apache Iceberg Table Maintenance](https://dev.to/jonisar/the-real-cost-of-apache-iceberg-table-maintenance-3kg)** — quantifies orphan-data storage waste, a ~20x query-cost gap between sorted and unsorted tables, and a ~10x compute-cost gap between Spark and purpose-built compaction engines.
- **[Data Platform Engineering]** **[Postgres on NVMe: performance and the convergence of transactions and analytics](https://clickhouse.com/blog/postgres-on-nvme)** — shows how local NVMe changes Postgres transaction performance, and where ClickHouse still remains necessary as analytical workloads scale.
- **[Analytics Engineering]** **[The Table Was Correct but Nobody Used It](https://blog.dataengineerthings.org/the-table-was-correct-but-nobody-used-it-9c621e34f06a?source=rss------analytics_engineering-5)** — on the gap between a technically correct table and one people actually rely on to make decisions.
