# A debugging diary: four late rows teach more about Iceberg than a clean batch

A quiet day for data engineering: the signal came from two practitioner write-ups on Iceberg late-arriving data and the recurring Spark-vs-DuckDB/Polars cost debate, while releases were limited to routine Airflow provider patches. No community discussion surfaced in today's window.

## Top stories
- **[Four Late Rows Taught Me More About Apache Iceberg Than 70,272 Clean Ones](https://medium.com/@adams-chang/four-late-rows-taught-me-more-about-apache-iceberg-than-70-272-clean-ones-44dca4a2eb4c?source=rss------data_engineering-5)** — Medium. A side-project postmortem: loading a year of clean weather data into Apache Iceberg was trivial, but a second batch with four late-arriving rows exposed the harder edges of the table format. *Why it matters:* late data is the common case in production, not the edge case, and these are the failure modes that clean benchmarks never surface.
- **[Is Spark Overkill for Most Pipelines? The Rise of DuckDB and Polars](https://medium.com/@whiteSign/is-spark-overkill-for-most-pipelines-the-rise-of-duckdb-and-polars-48a1fb5ecf16?source=rss------data_engineering-5)** — Medium. Argues most pipelines under 100GB don't need a cluster, backed by current AWS and Databricks pricing comparisons against single-node DuckDB and Polars. *Why it matters:* as single-node engines mature, the default choice of Spark for small-to-mid-size workloads is increasingly a cost decision, not just a technical one.
