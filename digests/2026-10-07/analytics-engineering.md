# Governed semantic layers are shipping, but AI agents keep skipping them

The strongest thread this window is a running disconnect between semantic-layer investment and how AI agents actually fetch context, surfaced across several wire pieces. Elsewhere, two substantive dbt essays on model correctness and the "why dbt won" argument stand out; the rest of the window was dominated by a beginner-level dbt tutorial series and vendor press on Dell's AI data platform.

## Top stories
- **[Companies are building governed semantic layers. Most say their AI agents are getting context somewhere else](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMi0gFBVV95cUxPWjllM2V1NTFFNFNlem8xNGZlRy1nVWxFdFc3YTlRXzRyYThXSVY3LWNjUzNVb21RMDVYZGlDdElXV2U2VFFfU0FSSExpdDFuS0JUT3Q2NlY4SlZKb0NqdV9pbFc1YU45UnRrbXE2NTh0ZXFoWjJ5VzhWX2NUYVdRYVpnUVhZNk9fcEJkM2NNYnpSd1dobzBVSXhadzAxcVdFVVdoMVBjNWYwRFZBYm9SekZrSkZ1aXpIUWRZeDZmNF94MGM2enZpR2VpUkFGYVlUS2c?oc=5)** — VentureBeat (Google News). Teams that invested in governed dbt/semantic-layer metrics are finding their AI agents pull context from elsewhere instead, a gap a separate Google News wire piece frames as "semantic layers may not be read by AI agents." *Why it matters:* the semantic layer's core promise, one governed definition everywhere, breaks down if the agents answering questions never actually query through it.
- **[The SQL Looked Right. The Number Wasn't.](https://medium.com/@seanlukeholland/the-sql-looked-right-the-number-wasnt-06f92cbd6ec2?source=rss------analytics_engineering-5)** — Medium. Walks through four modeling decisions behind a SaaS retention model built in dbt, arguing they matter more now that AI tools are generating a growing share of the queries. *Why it matters:* a concrete checklist for the specific case where the person reviewing SQL correctness didn't write it.
- **[dbt Didn't Invent Anything New — It Just Made SQL Respectable Again](https://medium.com/@gandrav07/dbt-didnt-invent-anything-new-it-just-made-sql-respectable-again-715c27671a87?source=rss------dbt-5)** — Medium. Argues dbt's real contribution was packaging existing SQL and version-control practice into something teams would actually adopt, not inventing new transformation theory.
