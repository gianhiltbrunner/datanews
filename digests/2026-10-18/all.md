# A quiet day across data: practitioner deep-dives outweigh vendor news

No major product launches or acquisitions landed today. Instead, the strongest stories came from practitioners working through hard operational and architectural questions: where Postgres' vector search hits a wall, how ClickHouse handles a single inserted row end-to-end, a Databricks streaming job that hangs after 8-12 hours, and whether anyone should still be hand-writing incremental transformation logic.

## Top 5 across domains
- **[Data Engineering]** **[The 'Just Use Postgres' Vector Cliff: Where pgvector HNSW Breaks at 50M Rows](https://dev.to/asifmiqbal/the-just-use-postgres-vector-cliff-where-pgvector-hnsw-breaks-at-50m-rows-417m)** — unpartitioned pgvector HNSW indexes need roughly 2x raw vector size in RAM and start failing on write amplification and recall once collections reach 20M–50M embeddings.
- **[Data Platform Engineering]** **[Structured Streaming job on a job cluster ends with "driver unresponsive" after 8-12h](https://www.reddit.com/r/databricks/comments/1x87u6t/structured_streaming_job_on_a_job_cluster_ends/)** — a detailed account of an Azure Databricks driver hanging despite a healthy 101GB heap, across multiple Structured Streaming queries sharing one driver.
- **[Data Engineering]** **[Follow one row: what ClickHouse does with your INSERT](https://dev.to/maksim-gr/follow-one-row-what-clickhouse-does-with-your-insert-g83)** — traces a single row from INSERT through merging, deduplication and deletion to explain recurring confusion around ReplicatedReplacingMergeTree and "Too many parts" errors.
- **[Analytics Engineering]** **[Who writes your is_incremental() block now?](https://medium.com/@shaantanutripathi/who-writes-your-is-incremental-block-now-87aa5653b014?source=rss------dbt-5)** — reacts to the Enzyme paper (SIGMOD 2026) on automating incremental transformation logic, from the perspective of someone who still hand-writes MERGE logic.
- **[Data Platform Engineering]** **[Data Platforms Are Getting Easier to Use. But Who Is Responsible When They Get It Wrong?](https://medium.com/@chameleon13/data-platforms-are-getting-easier-to-use-but-who-is-responsible-when-they-get-it-wrong-9373f0c1e31a?source=rss------data_platform-5)** — argues that as Google, Snowflake and Databricks abstract away more of the underlying mechanics, accountability for mistakes gets harder to pin down.
