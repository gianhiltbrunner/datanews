# Oracle pushes a semantic layer for AI agents as dbt Core becomes dbt OSS

Oracle dominated the newswires with a business semantic layer and AI catalog for its Autonomous AI Lakehouse, arguing that agents need structured business context to stop misreading data. Closer to the ground, dbt Labs' rename of dbt Core to dbt OSS came with an independent benchmark, and a widely shared opinion piece argued modern warehouses have made the Kimball star schema optional. A practitioner write-up on reprocessing BigQuery incrementally rounded out the day's substantive reading.

Oracle announced a business semantic layer, an AI planning engine and an AI catalog for its Autonomous AI Lakehouse, framed as stopping agents from misreading business data, alongside expanded Apache Iceberg support; the story ran across a dozen outlets (InfoWorld, TradingView, Tech Times, OpenTools, marketscreener.com and others) between 26 and 27 October. *Why it matters:* a major vendor explicitly building a semantic layer as the thing that grounds AI agents in business meaning — the exact argument analytics engineers have made about BI tools for years.
- **[dbt Core 2.0 Is Now dbt OSS: 5x Faster Parsing in My Test, and 8 YAML Habits That Break](https://medium.com/data-science-collective/dbt-core-2-0-is-now-dbt-oss-5x-faster-parsing-in-my-test-and-8-yaml-habits-that-break-4c7bf089a684?source=rss------dbt-5)** — Data Science Collective (Medium). dbt Core is now branded dbt OSS and remains Apache-2.0 after the Fivetran merger; the author's own 2,000-model benchmark found roughly 5x faster parsing in dbt-oss versus dbt-core 1.12, plus eight YAML habits that break under the new parser. *Why it matters:* independent, numbers-backed confirmation of a major rename and performance claim that dbt Labs itself hasn't fully substantiated in public yet.
- **[The Death of the Star Schema: Why Modern Warehouses Swapped Kimball for One Big Table (And Why…)](https://medium.com/@harsh1995hg/the-death-of-the-star-schema-why-modern-warehouses-swapped-kimball-for-one-big-table-and-why-5be063a79ef9?source=rss------dbt-5)** — Medium. Argues that cheap compute and columnar storage have pushed many modern warehouses toward wide, denormalized "one big table" models instead of Kimball-style star schemas. *Why it matters:* a deliberately provocative framing of a modelling debate every analytics engineer eventually has with their warehouse bill.
- **[Reprocessing BigQuery at Scale: The Hidden Failure Modes of Incremental dbt Pipelines](https://medium.com/dbt-for-analytics/reprocessing-bigquery-at-scale-the-hidden-failure-modes-of-incremental-dbt-pipelines-b913cf3fd847?source=rss------dbt-5)** — dbt for Analytics (Medium). Walks through designing deterministic backfills, safe partition replacement and dependency-aware recovery for incremental dbt models on BigQuery. *Why it matters:* incremental dbt models are easy to build and easy to silently corrupt during a reprocess; this lays out the failure modes before you hit them in production.
