# Airbnb's Chronon push mode cuts recommendation staleness from two days to seconds

A quiet day for original data-engineering writing: the Google News and Medium feeds were dominated by beginner Databricks walkthroughs, interview-prep listicles and job postings. The clearest signal came from Airbnb's production rollout of near-real-time feature updates and continued follow-up coverage of DuckDB 2.0's upgrade risks. Reddit and Bluesky carried the more technical discussion, from a GPU-accelerated DataFusion query engine to critiques of AI-driven infrastructure writing.

## Top stories
- **[Airbnb Cuts Recommendation Staleness from 2 Days to Seconds with Chronon](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/10/airbnb-chronon-recommendation/?utm_campaign=infoq_content&utm_source=infoq&utm_medium=feed&utm_term=AI%2C+ML+%26+Data+Engineering)** — InfoQ. Airbnb extended its Chronon feature platform with Push Mode and Near Real-Time Model Transform, cutting sequence-based recommendation embedding staleness from nearly two days to under a minute, improving offline NDCG by 1.67% and increasing uncancelled bookings by about a third of a percent in online testing. *Why it matters:* a concrete, measured example of moving a feature platform from batch to near-real-time without re-architecting the whole recommendation stack.
- **[DuckDB 2.0 for Data Engineers: Upgrade Risks and What to Know](https://dev.to/datadriven/duckdb-20-for-data-engineers-upgrade-risks-and-what-to-know-127n)** — DEV. A practical look at what changes with DuckDB 2.0.0 LTS ("Cinnamon Teal"): client-server mode, async I/O for object storage, a VARIANT type, triggers and a new parser, alongside a file format older versions can't read and some changed SQL behaviour. *Why it matters:* flags the specific upgrade pitfalls — not just new features — that teams should check before moving to 2.0.
