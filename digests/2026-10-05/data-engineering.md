# Why AWS bought DuckDB Labs, not Polars — and a 99.9% silent data-loss bug

Today's strongest signal is a community read on the logic behind AWS's DuckDB Labs acquisition, alongside two incident write-ups about pipelines that looked healthy while quietly failing. Elsewhere, most of the window was filled with Medium tutorials, stock-adjacent news noise, and routine release-candidate bumps.

## Top stories
- **[AWS acquires DuckDB Labs. Not Polars.](https://dataengineeringcentral.substack.com/p/aws-acquires-duckdb-labs-not-polars)** — Data Engineering Central. An analysis piece lays out why AWS chose to acquire DuckDB Labs rather than Polars, framing it as a signal of where corporate backing for open-source embedded engines is heading. *Why it matters:* vendor backing decisions like this shape which open-source engines get long-term investment and which get left to the community.
- **[A cache key ate 99.9% of my records and the pipeline looked green](https://dev.to/kyien/a-cache-key-ate-999-of-my-records-and-the-pipeline-looked-green-37i0)** — DEV. A cache-key bug silently dropped roughly one record in a thousand while every job-level metric still reported success, because monitoring counted runs rather than rows; the post outlines three habits meant to make that failure mode structurally impossible. *Why it matters:* it's a sharp reminder that "the job finished" and "the job did its job" are different claims, and pipeline health checks need to verify the latter.
- **[The fastest Glue job is the one you never start: removing per-job overhead from a 191-job pipeline](https://dev.to/kiran_gunturu_022085015ec/the-fastest-glue-job-is-the-one-you-never-start-removing-per-job-overhead-from-a-191-job-pipeline-aia)** — DEV. A follow-up to a prior Glue-tuning post describes cutting cost on an Oracle-to-vendor-feed pipeline (five Step Functions-orchestrated layers, 191 jobs) not by tuning individual jobs but by reducing how many times jobs are started at all. *Why it matters:* shows that orchestration-level redesign can beat micro-optimizing individual job configs for cost on per-DPU-hour billing.

## Worth reading
- **[Importing Data using Java Table Functions](https://duckdb.org/2026/10/05/import-data-with-java.html)** — DuckDB Blog. Walks through why a pure-Java DuckDB table function ended up being the method the team shipped for importing a year of operational data, after other approaches proved too slow.
- **[DuckDB vs Polars vs Spark on Databricks Serverless with Joins and Window Functions.](https://dataengineeringcentral.substack.com/p/duckdb-vs-polars-vs-spark-on-databricks)** — Data Engineering Central. Benchmarks the three engines specifically on join- and window-function-heavy workloads running on Databricks Serverless.
- **[How AI can investigate a 12 GB export without reading it into a prompt](https://dev.to/bigdatasight/how-ai-can-investigate-a-12-gb-export-without-reading-it-into-a-prompt-3plf)** — DEV. Argues that pairing an AI assistant with a real query engine — rather than loading data into the prompt — only works if the engineer first supplies the business definition of what a row means.
