# Coding agents refactor 300K lines of C in three weeks, but the metric is disputed

A case study of agentic refactoring at scale drew the day's sharpest practitioner debate, while Databricks shipped a first-look-worthy Shopify connector and a GraphRAG postmortem laid out where retrieval routing actually breaks. Robin Moffatt's monthly link roundup and a data-architect field guide round out a day otherwise dominated by AI-adjacent think pieces and career-anxiety posts.

## Top stories
- **[Agents Refactor 300K Lines in Three Weeks, and Practitioners Ask What It Proves](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/09/agentic-refactoring-case-study/?utm_campaign=infoq_content&utm_source=infoq&utm_medium=feed&utm_term=AI%2C+ML+%26+Data+Engineering)** — InfoQ. CodeScene had coding agents refactor 300,000 lines of C over three weeks for roughly $4,000 in tokens, verified by a frame-by-frame replay harness, with the agents building a playbook of codebase-specific recipes along the way. *Why it matters:* practitioners are pushing back on the scope and metrics, a useful check on how much of the result depends on the harness rather than the agents.
- **[Shopify Data in Databricks: A First Look at the New Lakeflow Connect Shopify Connector](https://dev.to/lucy1/shopify-data-in-databricks-a-first-look-at-the-new-lakeflow-connect-shopify-connector-g2l)** — DEV. Databricks' new managed Shopify connector in Lakeflow Connect, released in Beta on September 22, 2026, pulls products, orders, customers, inventory, fulfillments and discounts into Unity Catalog tables with incremental syncs. *Why it matters:* removes a common category of hand-rolled API extraction code, with a few Beta limitations worth planning around before production use.
- **[Graph RAG: where it actually breaks](https://dev.to/hannune/graph-rag-where-it-actually-breaks-59ok)** — DEV. A practitioner's Neo4j-based GraphRAG build held up fine once the schema and Cypher traversal were in place; the recurring failure mode was routing — deciding whether a question needs graph traversal, vector search, or both — which silently returns confident but incomplete answers instead of an obvious error. *Why it matters:* a concrete failure pattern for anyone combining graph and vector retrieval in production.
